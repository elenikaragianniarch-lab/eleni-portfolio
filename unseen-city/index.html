The Unseen City

A thesis on what the systems that measure cities leave out. It calls this the politics of noise, and argues for AI that states its point of view instead of claiming to be neutral.

Year
2026
Type
MArch thesis, the Bartlett, UCL
Supervisor
Elizabeth Selby
Award
Theory Report Prize, Bartlett Urban Design 2026, the programme's single thesis prize

Every system that measures a city has already decided what doesn’t count.

The argument

  1. Cities are governed by making them readable, and every reading leaves something out.
  2. What falls below a system's threshold gets built around as if it wasn't there. The threshold is always a decision.
  3. Rather than neutral machines, we should design systems that declare their partial point of view, and keep people responsible for them.

Tested on

MIT's Streetscore. 208,738 comparisons by 7,872 people, and about a million street views scored. Its scores track income and fail on unusual scenes.

My own Nine Acts. Including a critique of it: the agents who argued most also built tallest.

Why it matters

Building AI and thinking critically about it are usually separate jobs. This thesis is the other half of Nine Acts: what dataset choices, thresholds and evaluation do to a city.
