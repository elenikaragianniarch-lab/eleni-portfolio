Turin Machine

Two AI characters walk through Turin and talk: Nietzsche, who lived there, and Franco, the blind detective from Dario Argento's The Cat o' Nine Tails. Their conversations are boiled down to a vocabulary of 42 words, and each word changes a 3D form, so a sentence can be built as a space.

Year
2025
Role
Individual project
Context
MArch Urban Design, RC11, the Bartlett, UCL

Datasets

2,054 industrial buildings, 205 British Pathé films, 958 films shot in Turin, and a two-hour walk I recorded across 23 locations, cut into 30 sound fragments. The two characters only know the city through this material.

Two ways of seeing

Franco reads the city through sound, touch and uncertainty. Nietzsche reads it through concepts, critique and feeling. They walk the same 23 locations, and each exchange is grounded in what the datasets hold about that place.

From talk to words

TF-IDF over all the dialogues gives a vocabulary of 42 words. Each word has one of three behaviours: soft (low density, continuous), medium (balanced, partly broken) or chaotic (dense, fragmented). In Blender Geometry Nodes, each word sets density, fragmentation and continuity.

“The monumental industry hum feels tired; as brick and stones become unforgiving under the sun.”

Prints and spaces

The assemblies were 3D-printed and rendered as interiors.

A branch I dropped

I first clustered all the images with a self-organising map. It was technically sound but gave flat, unconvincing space, so I moved to the language-driven method above. The dropped branch is documented on the process blog.

How it works

  1. Data. Scraped building, film and archive datasets, plus audio segmentation of the recorded walk.
  2. Dialogue. Two persona agents (GPT-4o mini) talk at each location, constrained by the datasets.
  3. Vocabulary. TF-IDF over the dialogues, cleaned to 42 words in three behaviours.
  4. Geometry. Each word drives parameters in Blender Geometry Nodes; a sentence becomes a stack of forms.
  5. Output. 3D prints, renders and a short film.

Python, GPT-4o mini, TF-IDF, CLIP, SOM, audio segmentation, Blender Geometry Nodes, 3D printing, DaVinci Resolve
